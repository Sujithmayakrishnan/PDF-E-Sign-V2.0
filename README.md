What's new in v2.0:

Free digital signature — a genuine cryptographic signature (PAdES-based), generated with a certificate created on your own computer, at zero cost. No paid certificate authority, no subscription.

Tamper-evidence — any edit made to a signed PDF after signing is detectable. This is real public-key cryptography, not just a visual stamp.

Built-in verification — a new "Verify PDF Signature" tool checks who signed a document, when, and whether it's still intact, directly in the app.

Works alongside the visual stamp — the v1.0 signature image and Name/Place/Date/Time stamp still work exactly as before; the digital signature is an optional layer on top, not a replacement.

Still evolving — timestamp authority support, hardware token/smartcard signing, and PDF permission controls are on the roadmap for future versions.

Where this fits internally: NAAC accreditation files, internal office memos, faculty approval letters, audit trail documentation, course review sign-offs — anywhere a department needs a verifiable, dated record without routing every document through a paid signing platform or a physical signature-scan-upload cycle.

Safety, by design: documents and signature images never leave your computer — no cloud upload, no account required. The digital signature certificate is password-protected and stored locally. Tampering with a signed PDF after the fact is detectable, not just theoretically possible to catch. Every optional feature (internet time verification, GPS) fails gracefully — the app works fully offline if preferred.

Built as a lightweight, purpose-fit tool rather than a heavyweight platform — proof that solving your own institution's problems doesn't require buying someone else's software.
